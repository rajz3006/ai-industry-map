## Key Question 1: Current P/E, revenue growth, FCF, and ROIC-vs-WACC for each name, with a moat note

### Takeaway
Across the 17 names there is a clear split: mega-cap/established chipmakers and hyperscalers (Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Broadcom, Oracle, AppLovin) combine double-digit-to-triple-digit revenue growth with actual GAAP profitability and (mostly) positive free cash flow, while a second group (SoundHound AI, C3.ai, CoreWeave, BigBear.ai) has no trailing P/E at all because there are no trailing earnings, and negative free cash flow that is large relative to revenue. A third group (Palantir, Arm Holdings, AMD, Astera Labs, Super Micro Computer) is profitable and growing but carries valuation multiples (100x–300x trailing earnings) that are very hard to reconcile with current-period cash generation, even though the underlying business is real.

### Inferences
- **ROIC-vs-WACC benchmark used:** the semiconductor industry's benchmark WACC for 2026 is estimated at 10.55% (down slightly from 10.76% in 2025); the broader technology sector WACC range is estimated at 8.5%–12.0% — [calcmastery.com, "WACC by Industry (2025–2026)"](https://www.calcmastery.com/benchmarks/wacc-by-industry/). Using ~10.5% as the semiconductor-sector WACC reference point: Nvidia (ROIC 92–145%), Broadcom (30.68%), and Arm (14.16%) and Super Micro (14.03%) all clear it, while **AMD's ROIC of 9.77% is below its own sector's WACC estimate** — a specific, numbers-backed case of a company trading at a 165x trailing P/E while not yet earning its cost of capital on a trailing basis.
- Combining P/E with ROIC-vs-WACC and FCF conversion, a rough three-bucket placement is: **Bucket A (fundamentally grounded/near-value):** Nvidia, Broadcom, Alphabet, Oracle, and arguably AppLovin (pending FCF confirmation) — all show double-digit-to-80%+ growth, clear profitability, and (Alphabet/Broadcom/Nvidia) ROIC well above WACC with moderate-to-reasonable forward multiples. **Bucket B (expensive but real/growing profitability):** Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Palantir, Astera Labs — all profitable with real and often-growing FCF, but trading at multiples (or, for Amazon, with FCF now negative on a TTM basis due to capex) that price in a lot of continued execution. **Bucket C (primarily narrative/momentum):** SoundHound AI, C3.ai, CoreWeave, BigBear.ai, and — on valuation-vs-current-fundamentals grounds specifically — AMD and Arm Holdings, whose triple-digit P/E multiples (165.76x and 301.80x trailing, respectively) are hard to justify against current ROIC that is at or below sector WACC and FCF that is thin relative to market cap.
- Palantir and Astera Labs are the two hardest "swing" cases: both have genuinely strong, growing current profitability and cash flow (PLTR: 54.55% FCF margin; ALAB: net income +268.9% YoY), but their P/E multiples (101–187x forward-to-trailing) are themselves evidence of a narrative premium layered on top of real fundamentals — they are not speculation on zero profits, but they are not a "value screen pass" either.

## Key Question 2: Which names show the starkest disconnect between valuation multiple and current profitability/cash generation?

### Takeaway
The starkest, most numbers-backed disconnects are CoreWeave (negative FCF margin of -179.91% funded by $35.6B of debt, with customer concentration risk), C3.ai (a "Sell"-rated stock with revenue actually shrinking -37.5% YoY while still carrying an AI re-rating), and — among profitable, non-speculative companies — AMD and Arm Holdings, whose triple-digit trailing P/E ratios (165.76x and 301.80x) sit on top of ROIC that is at or below estimated sector cost of capital and free cash flow that is thin relative to market cap.

### Cited Findings
- **CoreWeave (CRWV):** trading on $48.41B market cap with TTM revenue of $7.59B, but TTM free cash flow of -$13.655B (FCF margin -179.91%), funded by $35.6B of total debt as of June 30, 2026, with the company reportedly directing roughly 25% of revenue to interest expense — [stockanalysis.com/CRWV](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/CRWV/), [stockanalysis.com/CRWV cash flow](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/CRWV/financials/cash-flow-statement/), [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/03/coreweave-stock-growth-debt-big-risk/)

- **AMD:** 165.76x trailing P/E despite ROIC of only 9.77%, which is below the ~10.5% 2026 semiconductor-sector WACC benchmark — i.e., on a trailing basis AMD is not clearing its own sector's estimated cost of capital, yet trades at one of the highest earnings multiples in the mega-cap/large-cap AI set — [stockanalysis.com/AMD ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/AMD/financials/ratios/), WACC benchmark via [calcmastery.com](https://www.calcmastery.com/benchmarks/wacc-by-industry/)
- **Arm Holdings:** 301.80x trailing P/E / 126.83x forward P/E, the highest multiple pair in the entire set, against TTM revenue growth of "only" 25.1% and an FCF yield of just 0.48% of its $317.41B market cap — meaning a company growing revenue in the mid-20s percent range is priced as if cash generation will scale enormously faster than it currently does — [stockanalysis.com/ARM](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ARM/), [stockanalysis.com/ARM ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ARM/financials/ratios/)

### Inferences
- The common thread across the starkest-disconnect names is not simply "unprofitable" (several profitable names — AMD, Arm — are on this list too), but **multiple-vs-current-cash-generation mismatch combined with either deteriorating fundamentals (C3.ai, BigBear.ai) or capex/debt-funded growth whose payback depends on a small number of counterparties' own uncertain economics (CoreWeave)**.
- CoreWeave is the single most quantifiable "circularity" risk case found in this research: its backlog depends heavily on OpenAI, which is itself guided to lose $115B before profitability, meaning CoreWeave's revenue visibility is tied to continued risk-tolerant spending by an unprofitable counterparty.

- Did not find dedicated 2026 financial-press commentary that explicitly bundles AMD or Arm into "AI bubble" discussions the way CoreWeave and C3.ai are routinely discussed — the AMD/Arm disconnect flagged above is this researcher's own ratio-based inference from stockanalysis.com data, not a claim already made by financial press.
