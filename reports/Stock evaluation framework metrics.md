# Building A Repeatable Eight-Step Stock Checklist

Every step of a disciplined stock-evaluation process turns out to have a concrete, sourceable metric attached to it — but almost none of those metrics carry a single universal threshold, because sector structure, capital intensity, and the business cycle change what "good" looks like at every step. Morningstar gates its moat rating on **ROIC exceeding WACC** with a 10-year (Narrow) or 20-year (Wide) durability test ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/morningstar-economic-moat-rating-3)); Piotroski's F-Score reduces financial-statement quality to nine binary checks scored 0–9, with 8–9 "strong" and 0–2 "weak" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotroski_F-score)); Beneish's M-Score flags likely earnings manipulation above a published **−1.78** cutoff ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beneish_M-score)); Wilder's RSI and ATR have exact 14-period smoothing formulas with 70/30 overbought/oversold guidelines he himself called non-absolute ([StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/relative-strength-index-rsi)); and Van Tharp's R-multiple framework shows a system can be profitable at a 30% win rate if winners average +15R against −1R losers ([Van Tharp Institute](https://vantharp.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/A_Short_Lesson_on_R_and_R-multiple.pdf)). The common thread is that every one of these frameworks is a *relative* or *conditional* test — against a company's own history, against sector peers, against the cost of capital, against a stop-loss distance — not an absolute number applied uniformly to every ticker. Applied to today's AI trade specifically, this discipline produces a sharp split: Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, Broadcom, and Oracle already carry the earnings, cash flow, and multiples of mature value compounders, while SoundHound AI, C3.ai, CoreWeave, and BigBear.ai show no trailing earnings and deeply negative free cash flow, and AMD and Arm Holdings sit in a hard-to-defend middle where triple-digit P/E multiples sit on top of ROIC at or below their own sector's cost of capital.

## Moat durability and cycle position set the boundaries of the thesis (Steps 1–2)

Before any number gets pulled, Buffett's circle-of-competence test is the gate: "the size of the circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital" ([Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/inside-warren-buffetts-circle-of-competence-concept/)), operationalized as a self-honesty checklist — can you explain the business model without quoting someone else's thesis, and have you reviewed your own track record for where mistakes cluster ([Coffee and Junk](https://coffeeandjunk.com/circle-of-competence/)). There is no numeric threshold here; it is qualitative, which is itself a finding worth preserving in a checklist rather than papering over with a fake score.

Moat analysis does have numbers. Morningstar's Economic Moat Rating requires a company to clear **ROIC > WACC** before it can earn a Narrow or Wide rating at all, then attributes the moat to one or more of five sources — network effect, switching costs, cost advantage, intangible assets, and efficient scale — with Narrow defined as defensible for roughly 10 years and Wide for 20+ years ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/morningstar-economic-moat-rating-3)). Pat Dorsey's popularized version in *The Little Book That Builds Wealth* condenses this to four sources (folding efficient scale into cost advantage) and gives each one a diagnostic test: network effects show up as margins that resist competition even as volume could migrate elsewhere; switching costs show up as multi-year customer retention (banking example cited at 6–7 years) despite competitive pressure; brand/intangible power requires demonstrated pricing power over a functionally equivalent substitute, not mere recognition; and the universal cross-check across all four is a **persistently high return on capital that does not erode toward industry-average levels over 10+ years** ([Anomaly Investments](https://anomalyinvestments.substack.com/p/pat-dorsey-the-moat-framework)). A practitioner-level numeric version of that cross-check: one secondary source proposes **ROIC above 15% sustained for 5+ years** as the point a moat becomes "plausible," **ROIC above 25% for a decade** as "wide-moat territory," gross-margin stability of **standard deviation under 3 percentage points over 5 years** as evidence of real pricing power, and **no single customer above 15% of revenue** as a diversification marker — all useful heuristics, but sourced to a single finance blog rather than a standards body, so a checklist should treat them as a starting rule of thumb, not settled doctrine ([Stock Alarm Pro](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/economic-moat-investing-guide)). The one hard legal tripwire here is real: under SEC Regulation S-K Item 101 and ASC 280-10-50-42, a company must disclose in its 10-K when any single external customer reaches **10% of total revenue** — a free, bright-line signal pulled directly from the filing's customer-concentration footnote ([Beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/11/customer-concentration-risk-10-percent-revenue-threshold-business-valuation-loan-capacity-negotiating-leverage-guide)).

Industry structure is read through market-concentration math. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (sum of squared market shares) and the four-firm concentration ratio (CR4, sum of the top four shares) are the two standard classifiers, but their "official" bands have actually shifted: the December 2023 DOJ/FTC Merger Guidelines presume a merger unlawful above **HHI 1,800 with a 100-point change**, a tightening from the 2010 guidelines' **HHI 2,500 "highly concentrated" / 1,500 "unconcentrated"** bands that are still widely taught in finance education ([Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2023/12/27/ftc-and-doj-release-finalized-merger-guidelines/); [Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/channels/microeconomics/learn/brian/ch-12-monopoly/mergers-and-the-herfindahl-hirschman-index-hhi)). CR4 is the more retail-practical substitute because it only requires the four biggest known competitors' revenue: **below 40% = fragmented, 40–60% = loose oligopoly, above 60% = tight oligopoly** ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/how-to-calculate-the-four-firm-concentration-ratio-cr4/)). Distinguishing secular growth from cyclicality is done by correlating revenue/EPS with the business cycle rather than a hard statistical cutoff — cyclicals (semiconductors, airlines, steel, autos, hotels) show revenue that tracks GDP, while secular growers are driven by structural shifts in customer behavior largely independent of where the economy sits ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/economics/cyclical-industry/); [Wall Street Mojo](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/cyclical-industry/)). For cyclicals, practitioners triangulate cycle position with a mosaic of free, public data rather than one number: the **ISM Manufacturing PMI** (September 2026 reading 54.5%, New Orders 55.3%) ([ISM](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/september/)), Federal Reserve **capacity utilization** (long-run average 75–80%, with 80–85% considered the "optimal" operating band) ([Federal Reserve G.17](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/capnotes.htm)), and, for semiconductors specifically, SEMI's **book-to-bill ratio** for manufacturing equipment, where a reading above 1.0 signals more orders received than shipped ([Wikipedia: Book-to-bill ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book-to-bill_ratio)). Howard Marks' explicit discipline in *Mastering the Market Cycle* is to read this mosaic rather than hunt for one "magic data point," shifting portfolio posture toward loss-avoidance when the mosaic reads late-cycle and toward opportunity-seeking when it reads early ([Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/how-to-invest-like-howard-marks-mastering-market-cycles/)). Peter Lynch's six-category taxonomy (Slow Growers, Stalwarts, Fast Growers, Cyclicals, Turnarounds, Asset Plays) is the natural output layer that routes Steps 1–2's findings into a single classification before valuation begins, since it explicitly ties the category to the appropriate holding period and valuation method ([Picture Perfect Portfolios](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/peter-lynch-six-stock-categories/)).

## Financial statement quality separates durable earnings from accounting artifacts (Step 3)

Reported revenue growth needs to be stripped of acquisitions, divestitures, and FX before it means anything; companies voluntarily disclose this bridge in non-GAAP reconciliation tables in the MD&A, but the adjustments are issuer-defined with no SEC-mandated format, so comparability across companies is limited and a company that stops disclosing the bridge, or changes its definition year to year, is itself a flag ([Aon plc 10-K, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315293/000162828026008116/aon-20251231.htm)). Margin analysis is formulaically simple — gross margin = (Revenue − COGS)/Revenue, operating margin = EBIT/Revenue, net margin = Net Income/Revenue — but "healthy" absolute levels swing enormously by sector, and even sector aggregators disagree with each other on the exact number:

| Sector | Gross margin | Net margin | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS/packaged software | 70–85% | 12% median (top-quartile >25%) | [Vena](https://www.venasolutions.com/blog/average-profit-margin-by-industry); [calcmastery](https://www.calcmastery.com/benchmarks/net-profit-margin-by-industry/) |
| Semiconductors | 55–59% | 30–41% (cyclically sensitive, sources disagree) | [csimarket](https://csimarket.com/Industry/industry_Profitability_Ratios.php?ind=1010) |
| Retail (general/grocery) | 26–55% depending on sub-sector | 3–7% | [calcmastery](https://www.calcmastery.com/benchmarks/net-profit-margin-by-industry/) |
| Industrials | n/a | 8% median, 14% top quartile | [calcmastery](https://www.calcmastery.com/benchmarks/net-profit-margin-by-industry/) |

Banks don't use these ratios at all — they run on **Net Interest Margin** (2–5% overall, large national banks 2.0–3.0%) and **efficiency ratio** (non-interest expense ÷ revenue, below 60% "optimal," below 50% "exceptional") ([banksift.org](https://banksift.org/faq/what-is-a-good-nim-for-banks); [timvero.com](https://timvero.com/blog/bank-efficiency-ratio)). The governing principle across every source is the same: "the right benchmark is always the sector median — comparing a retailer's 25% margin to a SaaS company's 75% margin is inappropriate" ([basisreport.com](https://www.basisreport.com/resources/gross-margin-by-industry-sector-benchmarks)), so a checklist should track the 3/5/10-year *trend direction* against a named peer basket rather than hardcoding one number.

The single most mechanically important distinction in this step is **ROE versus ROIC**. ROE = Net Income ÷ Average Equity is structurally gameable by leverage and buybacks because shrinking the denominator (equity) mechanically raises the ratio even with zero operating improvement; ROIC = NOPAT ÷ Invested Capital (NOPAT = EBIT × (1−tax)) is capital-structure-neutral because it divides pre-interest income by total capital, debt plus equity. A worked example built directly from these formulas makes the mechanism concrete: a company with $150 EBIT, $200 debt at 5%, and $800 equity shows ROE of 13.1% and ROIC of 11.25%; after it borrows an additional $400 purely to fund a buyback (EBIT unchanged, equity falls to $400, debt rises to $600), **ROE jumps to 22.5% while ROIC stays exactly 11.25%** — a 72% relative ROE gain from financial engineering alone, with net income actually falling due to the added interest expense ([WallStreetPrep](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/return-on-equity-roe/); figures calculated from cited formulas). The actionable screen this produces: rising ROE alongside flat-or-falling ROIC, falling net income, and rising debt is a leverage/buyback artifact, not a quality signal. Value creation itself is judged by the **ROIC − WACC spread** — a spread of **2–3 percentage points is "strong," and a spread sustained above 5 points is "preferred,"** while ROIC below WACC means every dollar of growth destroys value ([dividend.school](https://www.dividend.school/p/roic-vs-wacc-how-value-creation-really)); Damodaran's own NYU Stern sector data (treated by multiple sources as the authoritative current reference point, though not independently re-verified here against the live spreadsheet) puts 2026 sector ROIC at roughly 41.8% for Information Technology, 22.2% for Health Care, 14.4% for Communication Services, and as low as 5.2% for Real Estate ([stockunlock.com](https://stockunlock.com/investing/what-is-a-good-roic/)).

Leverage and cash-conversion metrics are equally sector-gated. Net Debt/EBITDA below **1.0x** is low leverage and above **4.0x** is high for a typical corporate, but software companies often run near **0x–2.5x net cash** while utilities and REITs structurally run **3.0x–8.0x** on the strength of regulated, predictable cash flow ([umbrex.com](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-analysis/finance/net-debt-to-ebitda-ratio/)). Interest coverage (EBIT/Interest) across roughly 500 companies averages 24.26x but ranges from **37.0x in Information Technology down to 2.87x in Utilities** — a sector where a coverage ratio other sectors would treat as distressed is structurally normal because of cash-flow stability, not thin margin for error ([wisesheets.io](https://blog.wisesheets.io/average-interest-coverage-ratio-by-industry-2026-update/)). Free cash flow conversion (FCF = Operating Cash Flow − CapEx; FCF margin = FCF/Revenue) runs **15–35% for SaaS, 10–15% for a mature business generally, and 1–8% for energy/utilities** ([freecashflow.org](https://freecashflow.org/blog/free-cash-flow-margin-formula-benchmarks)); a persistent, growing gap between positive net income and weak-or-negative FCF is described as a genuine earnings-quality warning (receivables buildup, aggressive revenue recognition) for a mature company — but the identical pattern is normal, not a red flag, for a capital-intensive growth business like a semiconductor fab or cloud buildout reinvesting ahead of current cash generation ([financialmodelingprep.com](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/education/statements/how-to-detect-earnings-quality-erosion-via-cash-flow-statement)) — a distinction directly relevant to several AI infrastructure names discussed below.

Four named, formally published quality frameworks round out this step. **Greenblatt's Magic Formula** uses Return on Capital = EBIT ÷ (Net Fixed Assets + Net Working Capital) deliberately with pre-tax EBIT rather than NOPAT, "to put companies with different debt levels and tax rates on an equal footing" ([oldschoolvalue.com](https://www.oldschoolvalue.com/stock-valuation/the-magic-formula-return-on-capital/)). **Damodaran's earnings normalization** cleanses non-recurring and financing items and, for cyclicals, averages across a full commodity/economic cycle rather than forecasting the next one ([NYU Stern](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/adamodar/New_Home_Page/valquestions/normearn.htm)). The **Piotroski F-Score** is nine binary tests (1 point each, 0–9 total) spanning profitability (ROA>0, CFO>0, ΔROA>0, CFO>Net Income), leverage/liquidity (falling long-term debt ratio, rising current ratio, no new share issuance), and efficiency (rising gross margin, rising asset turnover), with **8–9 considered strong and 0–2 weak** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotroski_F-score)). The **Beneish M-Score** combines eight accounting-ratio indices (DSRI, GMI, AQI, SGI, DEPI, SGAI, LVGI, TATA) into M = −4.84 + 0.92×DSRI + 0.528×GMI + 0.404×AQI + 0.892×SGI + 0.115×DEPI − 0.172×SGAI + 4.679×TATA − 0.327×LVGI, with scores above **−1.78** flagging likely earnings manipulation (a stricter −2.22 cutoff also circulates) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beneish_M-score)). The related **Sloan accrual ratio** — (Net Income − Operating Cash Flow) ÷ Average Total Assets — flags **accruals exceeding +10% of total assets** as a low-earnings-quality signal that has historically preceded underperformance as accruals revert ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/glossary/sloanratio)). None of these tools are redundant: Beneish and Sloan use different inputs entirely, so a company flagged by both carries meaningfully more weight than one flagged by either alone.

## Valuation requires triangulating relative, historical, and intrinsic measures (Step 4)

Every valuation multiple has an exact formula, and every source that publishes a "cheap/fair/expensive" band for that multiple also warns the band is only usable after sector, growth, and capital-structure adjustment. Trailing P/E (Price ÷ TTM EPS) and forward P/E (Price ÷ forecast EPS) typically run **15x–30x** as a generic "normal" range ([Stock Rover](https://www.stockrover.com/metrics/valuation/)); EV/EBITDA strips out capital-structure and depreciation differences and runs roughly **8x–14x for mature companies, above 15x pricing in high growth** ([Stock Rover](https://www.stockrover.com/metrics/valuation/)); EV/Sales and Price/Sales are the fallback multiples for pre-profit companies, with real-world medians ranging from **0.36x for grocery retail to 11.2x for software and 5.1x for median public SaaS**, which is precisely why no single cross-sector P/S threshold means anything ([aggregated search synthesis]); Price/Book runs **1.5x–2.5x "normal" for asset-heavy financials/energy/utilities** where book value proxies economic value, versus being largely meaningless for asset-light software ([eqvista](https://eqvista.com/price-to-book-ratio-by-industry/)); and FCF yield (FCF ÷ Market Cap) above **8%** is historically associated with strong long-run returns, with the entire band shifting up roughly 2 points as the 10-year Treasury yield rises, since FCF yield explicitly competes with the risk-free rate ([onveston.substack.com, aggregated]). Peter Lynch's **PEG ratio** (P/E ÷ expected EPS growth rate) targets fair value near **PEG = 1.0** and flags sub-1.0 as a bargain candidate, but the ratio becomes "effectively unusable" once growth approaches zero (turning any sensible P/E into a PEG of 8+) or earnings are negative ([Wikipedia: PEG ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEG_ratio); aggregated search synthesis) — which is exactly the population of unprofitable AI-theme names discussed below, where PEG simply cannot be computed.

Two complementary comparison methods anchor the "is this multiple justified" question. The first is **own-history percentile**: rank the stock's current multiple against its trailing 5–10-year distribution, with the informal convention being **below the 20th percentile = cheap versus own history, above the 80th = expensive versus own history** ([aggregated, Koyfin methodology](https://www.koyfin.com/help/percentile-rank-snapshot-feature/)). The second is **peer comps**: build an 8–15-company peer set, pull EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, and P/E, benchmark against the peer median (preferred over the mean for outlier resistance), and explicitly attribute any premium or discount to differences in growth, margin, or risk rather than accepting the gap at face value ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/finance-frameworks/comparable-company-analysis/)). Aswath Damodaran publishes free, regularly updated sector-median P/E, EV/EBITDA, and revenue-multiple datasets on his NYU Stern page specifically to anchor this comparison with real current numbers rather than generic round-number heuristics ([Damodaran: PE Ratio by Sector](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/pedata.html)). Damodaran's broader contribution is the sharp distinction between **pricing** a stock (multiple-based, benchmarked to comparables, driven by market mood) and **valuing** it (DCF-based, driven by the business's own cash-flow fundamentals) — his argument is that most market participants, including professional sell-side analysts, default to pricing because it is less work, even though data abundance has eroded the edge that shortcut-based pricing once had ([via Motley Fool interview](https://www.fool.com/investing/2024/02/10/aswath-damodaran-on-the-difference-between-pricing/)). His DCF mechanics discount Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF = EBIT×(1−tax)×(1−Reinvestment Rate)) at WACC (CAPM cost of equity, risk-free rate + beta×ERP, blended with after-tax cost of debt), with one hard, non-negotiable constraint: **the terminal growth rate can never exceed the long-run nominal growth rate of the overall economy** — roughly the risk-free rate, around 2–3% for developed markets ([Damodaran: FCFF Model](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/eqnotes/fcff.pdf)). For growth software specifically, the **Rule of 40** (revenue growth % + FCF or EBITDA margin % ≥ 40) is the standard health screen, and Bessemer's 2024 **Rule of X** refines it by weighting growth 2–3x more heavily than margin — a company growing 25% at a 15% FCF margin scores 72.5 Rule-of-X points versus a 10%-growth/30%-margin company's 53, even though both hit an identical Rule-of-40 score of 40, which is exactly the kind of distinction a pure Rule-of-40 screen misses ([Bessemer Venture Partners](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-rule-of-x)).

## Management quality and chart discipline convert a thesis into a position (Steps 5–6)

Capital-allocation quality is judged, in Michael Mauboussin's framework, primarily through the lens of **ROIC versus WACC** — the correlation between a firm's economic spread (ROIC−WACC) and its EV/Invested-Capital multiple runs roughly **0.58**, meaning the market systematically rewards firms earning above their cost of capital, and "when ROIC is similar to WACC, growth doesn't matter much for value creation; when ROIC < WACC, every dollar of growth burns intrinsic value" ([Matthew Harbaugh](https://matthewharbaugh.substack.com/p/inside-michael-mauboussins-roic-framework)). Mauboussin's five published principles of good capital allocation — zero-based allocation reviewed annually, funding strategies rather than isolated projects, no capital rationing but a real hurdle rate, zero tolerance for continuing to fund bad growth, and explicit awareness of when the market is mispricing the business — give the qualitative frame, but the two concretely checkable anchors are the ROIC-WACC spread and **goodwill impairment frequency**: a single write-down may reflect bad luck, but multiple impairments across different deals within a few years is read as evidence a company "systematically overpays, overestimates synergies, or can't integrate what it buys," with one illustrative rule framing three or more write-downs inside five years as a broken M&A process (citing AOL/Time Warner's $54B and Kraft Heinz's $15.4B write-downs as cautionary scale) ([New Constructs](https://www.newconstructs.com/much-goodwill-red-flag-portfolio/); [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/goodwill-impairment/)). Buyback quality is judged by whether repurchases accelerated when the stock traded below its own intrinsic-value estimate (accretive to remaining holders) or continued/accelerated near valuation peaks (a transfer of value from remaining holders to sellers) ([Matthew Harbaugh](https://matthewharbaugh.substack.com/p/inside-michael-mauboussins-roic-framework)).

Insider activity is tracked through **SEC Form 4**, legally required within **two business days** of the transaction and mirrored free on openinsider.com ([WallStreetMojo](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/sec-form-4/)). The highest-signal pattern is a **cluster buy** — three or more distinct insiders making open-market purchases (transaction code "P") within roughly a 60-day window — which academic work (Lakonishok & Lee, as summarized by secondary sources) associates with materially stronger forward returns than isolated individual purchases ([OpenInsider](http://openinsider.com/latest-cluster-buys); [MarketTriage summary](https://markettriage.com/insider-trading-signals)). Sales executed under a pre-arranged **Rule 10b5-1 plan** carry far less informational value than discretionary open-market trades, because the decision was locked in before any current view of the stock could factor in — the plan exists specifically as an affirmative defense against insider-trading liability, and is used overwhelmingly for sales rather than purchases ([Form4API](https://www.form4api.com/guides/10b5-1-trading-plans)). Insider ownership itself follows a U-shaped alignment pattern in one cited framework — alignment rising below 5%, an "entrenchment zone" roughly 5–25% where value can decline, and realignment above 25% — with practitioner convention converging on **roughly 5–15% as the sweet spot** for diversified companies and **10–30% for founder-led companies** ([UpsideInvest](https://www.upsideinvest.io/blog/how-to-find-stocks-with-high-insider-ownership)). Guidance track record is read not as "beats are good" but as a pattern check: companies that **consistently beat by a small, stable margin** have a healthy process, while large recurring beats are frequently evidence of deliberate "sandbagging" (guidance set 15–25% below likely actual results specifically to guarantee a beat) — a pattern academic work finds produces abnormal returns only in the year it starts, since the market eventually re-prices around it once recognized ([CLS Blue Sky Blog, Columbia Law School](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2023/07/07/does-lowball-guidance-work/?amp=1)).

Step 6's technicals have exact, codeable formulas with widely cited (if not universally standardized) thresholds:

|---|---|---|---|

| ATR (Wilder) | True Range smoothed over 14 periods | Stop = Entry ± (ATR × 1.5–3) | [Calculator Academy](https://calculator.academy/atr-average-true-range-stop-loss-calculator/) |
| Golden/death cross | 50-day SMA vs. 200-day SMA | Cross above = bullish, below = bearish | [SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/death-cross-chart-pattern/) |
| Bollinger Bands | 20-day SMA ± 2 standard deviations | Band touch/width signals volatility regime | [StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/bollinger-bands) |
| Weinstein Stage Analysis | 30-week SMA on weekly chart | Never buy below a declining 30-week MA | [TradingMomentum](https://tradingmomentum.substack.com/p/stan-weinsteins-stage-analysis-the) |

Dow Theory's volume-confirmation rule — volume should expand in the direction of the primary trend, and failure to expand is a warning the trend lacks conviction — underlies all of these ([StockCharts: Dow Theory](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/dow-theory)). Crucially, the research consistently frames technicals as execution-layer tools applied *after* a fundamental thesis is established, not as the thesis itself: "fundamental analysis answers 'what to buy'... technical analysis is a secondary consideration to optimize entry and exit points," and for value-oriented investors the preferred stop is a **fundamental thesis-break stop** (F-Score drops below 3, earnings yield turns negative, debt becomes a problem) rather than a pure chart-based stop whenever the two disagree ([Hard Money History](https://www.hardmoneyhistory.com/value-investing-technical-analysis/); [Quant-Investing](https://www.quant-investing.com/blog/stop-loss-strategies-complete-guide)). No canonical value investor (Buffett, Munger, Graham) was found on record explicitly endorsing or describing their own use of moving averages or RSI — this division of labor is a practitioner/quant-education synthesis, not a direct quote from a named value investor, and should be presented as such rather than attributed to Buffett himself.

## News context and composite scoring turn research into a sized position (Steps 7–8)

Information sources rank by structural accountability: **SEC filings** (10-K/10-Q/8-K "filed" items carry Exchange Act Section 18 liability) sit above **company IR materials and earnings-call transcripts** (management's own words, though 8-K Item 2.02 and 7.01 are "furnished" rather than "filed," with a lighter liability standard), above **sell-side analyst reports** (structurally biased toward buy/hold — sell ratings run as low as 0.5%–14% of all ratings issued) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18327)), above **mainstream financial press**, above **social media/forums**. For breaking news specifically, the highest-signal 8-K item types are **2.02 (earnings), 5.02 (executive/board changes), 1.01/2.01 (material agreements/completed M&A), 1.03 (bankruptcy), 3.01 (delisting), and 8.01** (catch-all), filed within four business days of the triggering event ([Wikipedia: Form 8-K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_8-K)). A specific, numbers-backed cross-reference technique used for semiconductor and infrastructure names: hyperscaler capex guidance functions as the dominant leading demand indicator, since the four major US hyperscalers purchased **$433.9B** of property and equipment in the four quarters through March 2026 — roughly **4x** the combined capex of TSMC, Samsung, Micron, and SK Hynix — with combined CY2026 hyperscaler capex guided at **$720–745B** and some analysts projecting **$1.2 trillion by 2027** ([siliconanalysts.com](https://siliconanalysts.com/market-data/hyperscaler-ai-capex); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/08/60886732/hyperscaler-capex-1-2-trillion-9-semiconductor-stocks-bofa)).

Mauboussin and Rappaport's **expectations investing** framework reverses standard valuation logic: rather than forecasting cash flows and comparing to price, it starts from the current price and reverse-engineers the market's implied expectations for sales, margin, and investment efficiency, then asks whether those embedded expectations are too high or too low ([Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/summary/expectations-investing-summary-michael-j-mauboussin-and-alfred-rappaport)). This has direct empirical support: stocks beating the informal "whisper number" gained an average of **more than 2%** on the announcement day, versus roughly **0.1%** for stocks that beat official consensus but missed the whisper number, because whisper numbers historically miss actual results by a narrower average margin (21%) than published consensus estimates (44%) ([Wikipedia: Whisper number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisper_number)) — meaning news should be judged against what the price already assumes, not against an absolute "good or bad" bar.

Composite scoring systems share one mechanical pattern — reduce several factors to a normalized score, then combine. **Greenblatt's Magic Formula** ranks a universe twice (by earnings yield = EBIT/EV, and by return on capital = EBIT/(Net Working Capital + Net Fixed Assets)), sums the two rank numbers per stock, and buys the lowest combined-rank names, typically held one year ([Stablebread](https://stablebread.com/magic-formula-investing/)). **Piotroski's F-Score** sums nine binary pass/fail tests into a 0–9 score. **O'Shaughnessy's Trending Value** filters to the top decile on a multi-ratio value composite, then re-ranks survivors by 6–12 month price momentum — his original 45-year backtest (1964–2009) claimed roughly a 10-point annual edge over the market, though a later out-of-sample replication found a **−10.2% annualized gap** versus that historical backtest, a useful caution that published composite-strategy CAGRs are upper-bound, not forward-looking ([whatworksintrading Substack](https://whatworksintrading.substack.com/p/what-works-on-wall-street-24-years)). **Stockopedia's StockRanks** score every stock 0–100 on Quality, Value, and Momentum as relative percentiles within the screened universe, explicitly defining a "Super Stock" as scoring above 60 on all three and a "Sucker Stock" as scoring below 40 on all three, and warning never to act on a high Value score alone without a Quality or Momentum "twin" (avoiding the classic value trap) ([Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/stockranks/)).

Position sizing closes the loop with two numerically linked conventions. The standard **risk/reward ratio** = (Target Price − Entry) ÷ (Entry − Stop), with **2:1 commonly cited as the floor and 3:1 as solid practice** — at 2:1 a system only needs to win roughly **34%** of trades to break even, and at 3:1 only **25%** ([Tradezella](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/risk-reward-ratio)). Van Tharp's **R-multiple** framework expresses every trade's outcome as a multiple of its initial planned risk (R = Entry − Stop): a worked example shows a system with a **30% win rate**, winners at **+15R** and losers at **−1R**, produces an expectancy of **(0.3×15) + (0.7×−1) = 3.8R per trade** — proof that expectancy, not win rate, determines long-run profitability, and that the risk/reward ratio and the R-multiple are the same number viewed before (planned) versus after (realized) the trade ([Van Tharp Institute](https://vantharp.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/A_Short_Lesson_on_R_and_R-multiple.pdf)).

## Which AI-theme stocks pass a value screen versus which are pure narrative

Running the Step 3–4 metrics above against 17 widely discussed AI-theme stocks (point-in-time figures as of **October 7, 2026**, via stockanalysis.com, cross-checked where noted against SEC filings) produces three distinct buckets, not a binary AI-bubble/no-bubble split.

**Bucket A — already-established value/quality compounders whose AI exposure is additive, not foundational.** Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, Broadcom, and Oracle were all large, profitable, moat-protected businesses before the generative-AI capex cycle intensified, and their current multiples reflect that lineage rather than pure narrative. Alphabet trades at the **cheapest trailing P/E of any mega-cap in the set, 17.44x**, on TTM revenue of **$445.87B (+20.1% YoY)** and FY2025 net income of **$132.17B (+32.0%)** ([stockanalysis.com/GOOGL](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/GOOGL/)). Microsoft trades at **29.49x trailing** on **$133.75B** TTM net income (+31.3%) ([stockanalysis.com/MSFT](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/MSFT/)). Broadcom combines **30.68% ROIC** with a **44.22% FCF margin** on revenue growing **48.7% YoY** ([stockanalysis.com/AVGO ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/AVGO/financials/ratios/)). Oracle trades at **22.70x trailing / 16.99x forward** on net income growing **52.1%** ([stockanalysis.com/ORCL](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ORCL/)). The one crack in this bucket: AI capex intensity is now large enough to flip even mega-cap free cash flow negative — Amazon's TTM free cash flow has gone **negative, −$11.6B (margin −1.50%)**, as capex rose to **$173.0B TTM** from $131.8B in FY2025, even though operating cash flow itself grew 33% ([stockanalysis.com/AMZN cash flow](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/AMZN/financials/cash-flow-statement/)) — meaning "quality compounder" status for this group currently has to be assessed on earnings and ROIC durability rather than on trailing FCF, which the AI buildout is temporarily suppressing industry-wide.

**Bucket B — real, growing profitability, but multiples that already price in a lot of continued flawless execution.** Palantir generates a genuinely strong **54.55% FCF margin, +96.54% YoY**, but trades at **164.27x trailing / 101.27x forward P/E** on a $464.63B market cap ([stockanalysis.com/PLTR](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/PLTR/)) — its cited 363.80% ROIC figure is a near-meaningless artifact of a tiny invested-capital base (large cash, minimal debt) rather than a literal 363% economic return, but the balance sheet strength it reflects is real. Astera Labs is profitable and growing fast (net income **+268.9% YoY**) but trades at **186.68x trailing / 67.54x forward**, with an **FCF yield of only 0.41%** of its $65.75B market cap — thin cash generation relative to valuation ([stockanalysis.com/ALAB](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ALAB/)). Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon also sit partly in this bucket on a pure multiple-vs-FCF-compression basis (Meta's FCF margin fell from 22.94% to 17.95% YoY) ([stockanalysis.com/META cash flow](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/META/financials/cash-flow-statement/)).

**Bucket C — primarily narrative/speculation, with specific numbers backing the disconnect.** CoreWeave shows the starkest, most quantifiable mismatch in the set: **TTM free cash flow of −$13.655B (margin −179.91%)**, funded by **$35.6B of total debt** as of June 30, 2026, with the company reportedly directing roughly **25% of revenue to interest expense**, and revenue concentration so severe that Microsoft alone was an estimated **~62% of 2025 revenue**, with Microsoft, OpenAI, and Meta together projected to exceed **85%** of 2026 revenue — a backlog partly dependent on OpenAI, a company itself guided to burn **$115B** before turning a profit ([stockanalysis.com/CRWV](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/CRWV/); [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/04/03/coreweave-stock-growth-debt-big-risk/); [electroneconomics](https://electroneconomics.substack.com/p/coreweaves-customers-are-becoming); debt figure corroborated against [CoreWeave's 10-Q, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000366/crwv-20260630.htm)). C3.ai is a "Sell"-rated stock (price target implying **~27% downside**) with TTM revenue actually **declining 37.5% YoY** and a FY2026 net loss **62.9% worse** than FY2025, despite still trading on an AI-re-rating narrative ([stockanalysis.com/AI](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/AI/)). SoundHound AI carries a **−70.07% FCF margin** on decelerating revenue growth (99.45%→54.6% YoY). BigBear.ai's 2025 revenue **declined 19.32% YoY** against a **$293.91M** net loss. And notably, two *profitable* names belong in this bucket on valuation-versus-cash-generation grounds alone: **AMD trades at 165.76x trailing P/E despite a 9.77% ROIC — below the ~10.55% estimated 2026 semiconductor-sector WACC** ([calcmastery.com WACC benchmark](https://www.calcmastery.com/benchmarks/wacc-by-industry/)), meaning on a trailing basis AMD is not yet clearing its own sector's cost of capital at one of the highest earnings multiples in the group; and **Arm Holdings carries the single highest multiple pair in the entire set — 301.80x trailing / 126.83x forward — against a 25.1% revenue growth rate and a 0.48% FCF yield** on a $317.41B market cap ([stockanalysis.com/ARM](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ARM/)). The AMD/Arm placement in Bucket C is this analysis's own ratio-based inference rather than a claim already circulating in financial press discourse, and should be read as such — it rests on directly sourced stockanalysis.com figures, but the "AI bubble" framing attached to it is original synthesis, not a repeated market narrative the way CoreWeave's and C3.ai's problems are.

A caveat applies across all three buckets: broader "AI bubble" discourse-level claims found in this research (EV/EBITDA near 25x for US tech/AI equities, a capex-vs-revenue-growth gap exceeding the 2001 telecom cycle, an unnamed "MIT study" claiming 95% of enterprise AI deployments show no measurable ROI) could not be traced to a specific primary study or report and should be treated as lower-confidence color, not load-bearing evidence — the per-company figures above, sourced to stockanalysis.com and cross-checked against SEC filings where noted, are the reliable basis for the bucket placement.

## What this means for a repeatable checklist

The clearest practical implication is that a usable per-ticker checklist cannot hardcode single numeric thresholds across steps 1–4 — it needs a sector lookup table (software vs. semiconductors vs. financials vs. utilities, at minimum) behind every margin, leverage, and multiple check, because the same raw number (a 2.5x Net Debt/EBITDA, a 55% gross margin, a 20x P/E) is unremarkable in one sector and a genuine red flag in another. The second implication is sourcing discipline: Damodaran's NYU Stern datasets, SEC EDGAR filings, and the Piotroski/Beneish/Greenblatt academic formulas are rigorous enough to anchor a checklist directly, while a meaningful share of the specific numeric thresholds cited across all eight steps — the 15%/25% ROIC moat thresholds, the 5–15% insider-ownership "sweet spot," the 90/70 composite-score guideline, the 20%/140% chart-overextension figures — trace back to practitioner blogs and trading-education sites rather than peer-reviewed or regulatory sources, and should be labeled in the tool as adjustable conventions rather than presented as settled law. Applied to the present moment, the discipline itself is the finding: the AI trade is not one trade. Six to seven names already clear a traditional value screen on their own pre-AI fundamentals; a second group is paying a real, quantifiable premium for continued flawless execution; and a third group — not limited to the obviously unprofitable names — is pricing in growth that current ROIC and cash generation do not yet support.
